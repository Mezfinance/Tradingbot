## What this repo is

A Python 3.10+ Claude-driven Solana memecoin scanner/trader. Paper trading is the default. It was built as a better version of the "Grok bots" people promote online. `RESEARCH.md` holds the findings: the viral returns are survivorship bias, and the realistic edge comes from risk control plus measurement.

**Naming (user, 11-05):** every quant (systematic trading) strategy belongs to **MZFq**: Supermeme (this bot's books) and MZF Short Only (`shortbook/`) are MZFq programs. Lending strategies (on-chain venture debt, the `mzf-protocol` repo) are **MZF Protocol**. Use these names in docs, reports and the dashboard.

Other docs: `DEPLOY.md` (Fly steps, canary arming), `FACTORS.md` (300 candidate pump factors), `SHRED_DECODER.md` (shred / router decoder design), `HYBRID_CAPTURE.md` (two-feed capture schema), `research/listings/STRATEGIES.md` (short-only fund strategies).

## Conventions and invariants

- Claude never sizes positions or sets stops. Keep risk logic deterministic in `risk.py`.
- Hard filters can't be overridden by the LLM. Safety-check failure means reject.
- Token names, descriptions and web content are untrusted. The system prompt says so; keep it that way.
- Every analysed token (buy or pass) is journalled, so the buy-vs-pass comparison stays unbiased.
- **pumpswap studies run only on pools we entered (user, 2026-11-16).** A pumpswap pool behaves differently when we interact with it than when we don't: our real buy is capital the operator reacts to — reactive LP pulls that scale with our trade size (`canary-slots`: pulls ~18 s after our fill, faster the bigger we go), and survivors tend to go flat once we enter (the wash appears to stop). So observational studies on untouched pools — ideal-fill reserve reads over `watch_launch_live`, `band-prize`, `hold-study`, `probe-exits`, `launch_filter`, the TP sweep, every "what-if at $X" — systematically overstate the edge and do not reproduce live (paper books showed +140% while real canary trades were negative). Any pumpswap study whose conclusion feeds a trading decision (sizing, a go-live, a new book) must use only pools we actually entered: real/canary trades, and feint/probe-touched pools. Observational studies generate hypotheses only — never a sizing or go/no-go call. **Caveat (user, 2026-11-16):** history *is* usable once we first identify which pools had *outside* participation. A pool that rugged while a non-operator wallet still held a balance carries on-chain marks of a trapped outside buyer — distinct non-wash buyer wallets, a held net-buy position through the LP pull, funding separate from the operator's cluster — and those pools are a large proxy for our own entered-pool regime (`canary-slots` says the pull reacts to outside *capital*, not our identity, so a generic outsider is a fair stand-in for us). So a study restricted to outside-interacted pools — ideally intersected with our own entry criteria, and validated by confirming our canary-entered pools carry the same marks — is a valid basis for rug-avoidance work. The labeling data largely exists (`watch_pool_wallets` fingerprint, `watch_wallets` funding, `launch_hist`/`helius.trades` streams). Pure-wash pools nobody outside touched stay excluded.

- **Watching a backgrounded log: never chain `tail -f` into another `tail`/line-counting filter** (user, 11-20). Python output buffering is already handled by running the script with `python -u` (the box's `absfast.py`/`absmove.py`/shred scripts), so a log watch that shows nothing is almost always a *pipe* bug, not a Python buffer: `tail -f LOG | tail -n 1` (and any `tail -f | <filter that waits for EOF>`) blocks forever, because the downstream filter only emits at end-of-stream and a `-f` stream never sends EOF. For a point-in-time read use plain `tail -n N LOG` (no `-f`), which returns at once.
